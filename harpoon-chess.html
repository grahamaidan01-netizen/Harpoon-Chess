Harpoon Chess

Standard chess, plus one addition: promote a pawn into a Harpoon and pull pieces — friend or foe — across the board.

Rating 1200

A chess variant designed by Aidan.

How the Harpoon works

Everything else is ordinary chess — check, checkmate, castling, en passant, the 50-move rule, all unchanged.

Getting a Harpoon

Standard Harpoon — promote a pawn to it, same as promoting to a queen. It's immobile and stays on that square for the rest of the game unless captured.

Horseback Harpoon — promote a pawn to a Harpoon while a friendly knight stands beside the promotion square (same rank, one file over). On your very next turn, move that Harpoon onto the knight's square to fuse: the knight is consumed, and the Harpoon can now move like a knight. Miss that one turn and the fusion is lost for good.

The pull

A Harpoon can pull one piece toward itself along a clear rank, file, or diagonal, once per turn.

Enemy piece: captured and removed. You may then revive one of your own previously-captured pieces of equal value (pawn = 1, knight/bishop = 3, rook = 5, queen = 9) onto the vacated square.

Friendly piece: simply relocated next to the Harpoon, along the line it was pulled from.

The king can never be pulled.

Movement

Standard Harpoon — cannot move at all. Pulling is its only action.

Horseback Harpoon — moves like a knight, and can still pull on the turns it doesn't move.

Both kinds can be captured normally, by any piece that can reach them.
